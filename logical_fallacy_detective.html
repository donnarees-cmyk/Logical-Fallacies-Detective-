What is a Logical Fallacy?

Welcome, Detective! An argument is when someone gives reasons to prove an idea. But watch out! Sometimes people use sneaky tricks called Logical Fallacies. A fallacy is a mistake in logic that makes an argument look strong, but it's actually broken!

💡 Detective Mission: Click any card below to listen to its case file. Learn all 4 fallacies to earn your "Logic Rookie" badge and unlock the Arena!

🤼

1. Ad Hominem

Person Attack

Definition: Attacking the person's looks, clothing, or traits instead of their actual idea.

💬 Example: "Don't listen to Alex's plan for the school recycling drive—he wears goofy socks!"

❌ Why it fails: Sock choice has zero connection to a recycling plan!

🎺

2. Bandwagon

Popularity Trick

Definition: Claiming an idea is right just because "everyone else" is doing it.

💬 Example: "You have to get these high-top sneakers! Everyone in 6th grade has them!"

❌ Why it fails: Just because something is popular doesn't mean it's smart or true.

🌾

3. Straw Man

Fake Distortion

Definition: Exaggerating or twisting someone's words to make them look ridiculous.

💬 Example:
• Leo: "We should eat more vegetables at lunch."
• Maya: "So you want to ban all snacks and force us to eat lawn grass?!"

❌ Why it fails: Maya invented a extreme fake argument Leo never made!

🛷

4. Slippery Slope

Extreme Chain

Definition: Claiming one small event will lead to a giant catastrophe without any proof.

💬 Example: "If I forget my pencil today, I'll fail the quiz, fail 6th grade, and never get a job!"

❌ Why it fails: One tiny mistake does not automatically cause total disaster!
